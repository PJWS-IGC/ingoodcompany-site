The rules that decide who can do this work in Ontario are being rewritten right now.

Funeral Homes & Directors Professional support Death literacy Family communication August 5, 2026

In late 2025 Ontario passed the Free Trade and Mobility Act, and in December the province published a draft regulation to put mutual recognition into practice. The Bereavement Authority of Ontario has flagged it and opened the door for the sector to comment. The mechanism is straightforward on paper. If you hold an authorization to provide a service in a reciprocating province, you would be entitled to the equivalent authorization here. For funeral, burial and cremation services, that means a director licensed in British Columbia or Alberta could, in principle, practise in Ontario without re-qualifying, and an Ontario director could do the same the other way.

This is worth the profession's attention, because death care has always been regulated province by province, and Ontario's framework under the Funeral, Burial and Cremation Services Act is among the most detailed in the country. Mutual recognition asks a real question. When a licence crosses a provincial line, do Ontario's consumer protection standards travel with the practitioner, or does the practitioner simply arrive under a lighter regime that Ontario now has to honour?

There is a version of this that is good for the field. A retiring director in a small northern town who cannot find an Ontario-trained successor might find one from Manitoba. Labour mobility can ease a workforce crunch that is already reshaping rural access. And there is a version that is not good, where harmonization drifts toward the lowest common standard and the protections Ontario families rely on quietly thin out to match everyone else.

The point is not to predict which way it goes. The point is that this is being decided during a consultation window, by the people who show up to it. A profession that pays attention to the regulatory ground it stands on should be in that conversation while it is still open, not reading about the outcome afterward.

If a licence earned in another province becomes a licence to practise in yours, what has to travel with it for families to be as protected as they are today?

Reading this from another corner of the field?

For anyone working under provincial death-care rules, the mutual-recognition question is whether Ontario's consumer protections travel with an out-of-province licence, and the consultation window is open now.

Source: Bereavement Authority of Ontario, Public feedback welcome on the province's Proposed Approach to Implementing Mutual Recognition under the Ontario Free Trade and Mobility Act, 2025; Government of Ontario draft regulation under OFTMA (December 15, 2025)
