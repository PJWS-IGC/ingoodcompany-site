Green Burial Is Growing and Being Fought. In Ontario the Fight Looks Different.

Community Educators Green burial Funeral planning Death literacy Meaningful goodbye August 2, 2026

CNN reported in May on a pattern showing up across North America. Communities are blocking natural burial proposals over fears about groundwater, property values, and wildlife, fears that environmental scientists say are largely unfounded. In Minnesota a judge dismissed a lawsuit brought by green burial operators against a county that had banned the practice. More than 500 cemeteries in the United States and Canada now offer green burial, up from roughly 100 in 2015, but the growth is uneven and the pushback is real.

For anyone who educates the public about death and dying, the useful distinction is that the Ontario story is not the American one. Here the barrier is rarely a neighbour with a petition. It is regulation. Human remains must be interred in a licensed cemetery, and a natural burial section has to sit inside one. The conservation burial grounds that exist in parts of the United States, where land is permanently protected through the act of burial itself, are not permitted under our Funeral, Burial and Cremation Services Act.

The result is quieter and just as limiting. A family in much of the province who wants a natural burial often finds the nearest option is 60 to 100 kilometres away. That is beginning to change. A green burial section at St. Stephen's Cemetery is set to open this spring, and Highlands East is planning one as well. The Green Burial Society of Canada certifies the sites that actually meet the standard, which matters, because the term gets used loosely by operators who want the language without the practice.

The teaching moment is that families are reading the same national coverage and arriving with the same question. Can I actually do this here? The honest answer in Ontario is yes, but not everywhere, and not yet close to home for most.

When families want a greener goodbye, are we educating them toward what is truly available to them, or toward what we wish were?

Reading this from another corner of the field?

For funeral directors, doulas, and celebrants, the practical service is an honest map: name the Green Burial Society of Canada's certified sites, be straight about the 60-to-100-kilometre reality, and design a committal that fits the setting.

Source: CNN, Green Burials Gaining Ground and Sparking Backlash (May 18, 2026); The Narwhal and Village Media reporting on Ontario green burial access; Green Burial Society of Canada; Ontario Funeral, Burial and Cremation Services Act (FBCSA)
