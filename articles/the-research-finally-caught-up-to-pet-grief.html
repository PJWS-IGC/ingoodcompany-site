The Research Finally Caught Up to Pet Grief.

Pet Loss Support Disenfranchised grief Complicated grief Professional support Death literacy July 19, 2026

A study published in PLOS One this January surveyed 975 adults in the UK and found that 7.5 percent of people who had lost a pet met the criteria for prolonged grief disorder. That rate is comparable to what follows the death of a sibling, a close friend or a partner. Among people who had experienced both pet and human loss, 21 percent said the pet loss was the most distressing bereavement of their lives.

The researchers also found the symptom structure of prolonged grief does not differ between pet and human loss. Grief does not check the species of the one who died.

This lands hard in Canada, where roughly six in ten households live with a dog or cat. The 2024 Canadian Pet Population Survey counted 7.2 million dogs and 8.2 million cats here. That is millions of significant deaths every year, most of them grieved without ritual, without leave from work, and often without anyone acknowledging that a loss occurred. Pet grief may be the most common disenfranchised grief there is.

The supports exist, but they are thin and largely unknown. The Ontario Veterinary College runs a pet loss support hotline, and OVC Pet Trust recently launched a free Pet Loss Support Resource Centre with guides, expert interviews and a documentary series on navigating pet loss. Every practitioner in this space should know these resources exist, because the family embarrassed to be grieving too much will not go looking on their own.

When someone tells you their hardest loss was an animal, does your practice have a place for that grief?

Reading this from another corner of the field?

For counsellors, veterinary teams, and anyone doing death literacy work, the finding is that pet grief carries the same symptom structure as human loss, so screen for it and keep the OVC pet loss hotline and OVC Pet Trust resource centre on hand.

Source: PLOS One (January 2026), No pets allowed: Evidence that prolonged grief disorder can occur following the death of a pet; The Conversation; CAHI 2024 Canadian Pet Population Survey; OVC Pet Trust
