Waterloo Region Just Built a Grief Hub. The Rest of Us Should Be Watching.

Hospice Care Professional support Hospice transition Death literacy Legacy & remembrance July 26, 2026

As of January 1, 2026, Bereaved Families of Ontario Midwestern Region no longer operates as a separate organisation. Its programs and operations have integrated with Hospice of Waterloo Region to form a single expanded Grief and Bereavement Program, running everything from general information through peer led groups to specialized children's programming and professional counselling. The boards of both organisations voted for it. BFO Midwestern had been doing peer based grief support in that region for more than thirty years.

The stated goal is a Grief Hub of Waterloo Region, described as a central point of connection where anyone navigating loss can find free programming and support at the right time. The commitments named in the announcement are specific enough to hold them to: expanding into rural townships, continuing specialized programming for children and youth, advancing equity and inclusion in grief care, and extending support to unhoused and marginalized community members. That last one is not standard language in this sector and it deserves to be noticed.

There are two honest readings of any integration like this. One is that a family in crisis stops having to guess which of four organisations is the right door, and the back office cost of running two charities becomes programming instead. The other is that a peer led model built by bereaved volunteers is now inside a clinically accredited hospice, and those two cultures do not automatically survive contact with each other.

Both readings can be true. What determines which one wins is whether the peer piece stays peer led rather than becoming a lower tier of a counselling service. Ontario has a growing number of small grief charities running on volunteer hours and precarious funding. More of them will face this decision in the next few years, and Waterloo just went first.

When a peer led grief organisation joins a clinical one, what has to be written down at the start to make sure the peer model survives it?

Reading this from another corner of the field?

For funeral directors, social workers, and doulas who keep the referral lists families actually receive, a single coordinated grief hub is easier to refer into than a list of four, and independent practitioners should introduce themselves to it early.

Source: Hospice Waterloo Region, Two Trusted Organizations Unite to Strengthen Grief and Bereavement Services Across Waterloo Region (November 17, 2025); Bereaved Families of Ontario, Midwestern Region; Hospice Palliative Care Ontario, Grief and Bereavement Supports
