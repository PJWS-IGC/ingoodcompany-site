Ontario Stopped Charging a Fee When a Baby Is Stillborn. It Took Until Last Year.

Perinatal & Pregnancy Loss Perinatal loss Disenfranchised grief Death literacy Professional support July 19, 2026

In April of last year, the Bereavement Authority of Ontario eliminated the business licensing fee it charged in relation to stillborn births. The province had already moved to remove the cost of obtaining a certified copy of a stillbirth registration. The BAO followed, ending the fees licensees paid for funeral services, transfers, burials and cremations for a stillborn child.

On paper this is a minor administrative change. The BAO is funded entirely by licensee fees, and this was one small line item. But anyone who has sat with a family after a stillbirth knows there is nothing small about what that fee represented. It said, in the quiet language of paperwork, that this death was a transaction like any other.

Removing it says something different. It says the province is learning to treat pregnancy and infant loss as real bereavement rather than a regulatory footnote. Families who lose a child at birth have long been asked to grieve inside a system that barely acknowledged their loss existed. Every place where that changes matters, even when the change looks like accounting.

For those of us in the sector, the work now is making sure the gesture becomes practice. A waived fee helps no one if a family never hears about their options, or meets a professional who treats a stillbirth arrangement as routine. The regulation caught up. The care around it is ours to deliver.

Where else in our systems are grieving families still being treated as paperwork, and what would it take to change it?

Reading this from another corner of the field?

For funeral directors, celebrants, labour-and-delivery nurses and hospital social workers, the practical move is to name the change to families and make sure a stillbirth is never met as routine paperwork, because the regulation only helps if the care around it does too.

Source: Bereavement Authority of Ontario notice (April 7, 2025, effective April 1, 2025); Government of Ontario news release (fall 2024)
