Ontario Fixed the Price List Problem. It Did Not Fix the Record.

Funeral Homes & Directors Funeral planning Death literacy Difficult conversations Professional support July 26, 2026

Six years ago the Auditor General of Ontario went funeral shopping. The audit team sampled 100 licensed operators of funeral homes, transfer services, cemeteries and crematoriums and found that only 26 posted a price list on their website. They sent mystery shoppers to the same 100. In half of those encounters the shoppers met sales pressure, misleading information, or both. When they asked what a basic cremation costs at the 70 operators offering one, the quotes ran from $512 in Windsor to $8,000 in Toronto.

Then the system worked, and that deserves saying out loud. By the 2022 follow up, the Bereavement Authority of Ontario had fully implemented the pricing recommendations. Any operator with a website now has to make a free, printable price list available in a clearly visible place. The templates are standardized across licence types and they separate what the law requires from what is optional. Unannounced inspections and mystery shopper visits became part of the regular toolkit. Fifty seven percent of the Auditor General's recommendations were fully implemented, with another twenty nine percent in progress.

What did not move is the part that would let a family check our work. The recommendation to publish the outcomes of inspections: little progress. The recommendation to give the public more detail on enforcement decisions and actions taken against licensed and unlicensed operators, with family privacy protected: will not be implemented. The recommendation to have a board member whose job is advocating for consumers: little progress. The Auditor General's follow up states plainly that these should still be done.

That is a strange place for a profession to sit. A family can now compare our prices and cannot see our record. For the operators doing this properly, and that is the overwhelming majority, opacity is not protection. It means the careful ones and the careless ones look identical from the parking lot.

If Ontario published inspection outcomes tomorrow, would your corner of this sector be better off or worse off, and what does your answer tell you?

Reading this from another corner of the field?

For the hospice social workers, doulas, celebrants and clergy who refer families to a funeral home, this gap is yours too: in the absence of a public record, you are vouching from experience, so know what you actually know about the places you recommend.

Source: CBC News, Ontario says consumers deserve better protection from high pressure sales tactics by funeral homes; Office of the Auditor General of Ontario, Value-for-Money Audit: Bereavement Authority of Ontario (2020) and Follow-Up (2022 Annual Report, Section 1.09)
