A Third of Canadians Report No Religion. Most Still Default to a Religious Funeral. That Gap Is Ours to Close.

Celebrants & Officiants Celebrant-led service Meaningful goodbye Memorial & ceremony Death literacy August 2, 2026

The 2021 census recorded that 34.6 percent of people in Canada report no religious affiliation, about 12.6 million people, up from 16.5 percent in 2001. In British Columbia it is 52 percent. In Yukon it is nearly 60. The country has changed faster than its funerals have.

Here is what I see, and I suspect you see it too. A family with no active faith still books a religious service, not because it reflects who their person was, but because it is the only script they know. They assume a funeral requires a member of the clergy, a set liturgy, and words that may not have meant much to the person who died. They default, because no one told them there was another way.

A celebrant-led service is that other way. It is not a lesser service or an absence of the sacred. It is a ceremony built around the actual life, the actual relationships, the actual voice of the person who died. For a family that has drifted from religion but still wants meaning, ritual, and a real goodbye, it is often the truer fit. The Certified Celebrants Association of Canada exists in part to make that option visible and credible.

The work here is not persuasion. It is information. Most families are not choosing religious over secular. They are choosing the known over the unknown, because the unknown was never explained to them.

If a third of the country no longer holds a religion, why do so many still assume the only real funeral is a religious one, and whose job is it to tell them otherwise?

Reading this from another corner of the field?

For funeral directors, doulas, and hospice teams, the fix is information at the decision point: offer a celebrant as an equal option to clergy, so a non-religious family designs a goodbye that matches their values rather than inheriting a template.

Source: Certified Celebrants Association of Canada (ccaoc.ca); Statistics Canada, 2021 Census of Population, religion data (released October 2022)
